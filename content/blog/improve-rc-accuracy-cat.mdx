## The 60% Trap

![RC explanation](/blog/image1.png)

You’ve solved more than 100 Reading Comprehension passages. You’ve watched videos, learned techniques, and even felt that your understanding has improved over time.

And yet, your accuracy refuses to move beyond 60%.

This is one of the most frustrating phases in RC preparation. You’re putting in effort consistently, but your scores don’t reflect it. It almost feels like you’re stuck in a loop.

> The problem is not effort.
> 
>The problem is where that effort is being applied.

---

## The Real Problem

Most students believe that RC is a test of English comprehension.

It is not.

RC is fundamentally a test of *decision-making under uncertainty*.

When you attempt a question, the challenge is rarely about understanding the passage. More often, it is about choosing the correct answer among multiple options that seem equally plausible.

The real problem is not understanding the passage — it is choosing correctly under pressure.

---

## What Actually Happens During RC

Let’s break down what typically happens inside your head when you attempt an RC question.

You read the passage and understand it reasonably well. The flow makes sense, the arguments are clear, and you feel confident.

Then you move to the options.

Suddenly, things change.

At least two options seem correct. You eliminate one option with some confidence, but now you are stuck between the remaining choices.

You reread the question. You go back to the passage. You try to validate each option again.

And this is where overthinking begins.

Eventually, you select an answer — often based on instinct rather than structured reasoning.

And that is exactly where accuracy drops.

This is not a comprehension issue.

This is a decision error.

## Why Most Aspirants Stay Stuck

Most aspirants try to improve RC by increasing volume.

They solve more passages. They consume more content. They believe that exposure alone will fix the problem.

But they rarely analyse how they are making decisions.

They don’t ask questions like:

- Why did I choose this option?
- Why did I reject the correct answer?
- What pattern is repeating in my mistakes?

Without this layer of thinking, practice becomes repetition — not improvement.

And that’s why accuracy plateaus.

---

## What Actually Improves Accuracy

Improvement in RC does not come from solving more questions. It comes from improving how you think while solving them.

You need to start observing your own decision process.

When you get a question wrong, don’t just look at the correct answer. Break down your thinking:

- What made the wrong option attractive?
- What signal did you miss in the correct option?
- Were you guessing or reasoning?

Over time, you’ll start noticing patterns in your mistakes.

And once you can see those patterns, you can fix them.

That’s when accuracy starts moving beyond 60%.

---

## Final Thought

RC is not about reading more.

It’s about thinking better.

The moment you shift your focus from *solving questions* to *understanding your decisions*, your accuracy will begin to improve.

And that’s the shift most aspirants never make.
