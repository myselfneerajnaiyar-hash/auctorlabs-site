## Why para summary is a scope test

A CAT para summary question does not ask you to rewrite a paragraph. It asks you to choose the option that preserves the paragraph's central claim, logical movement and degree of certainty. The wrong options are often factually related; they fail because they shrink, stretch or tilt the original argument.

This guide uses **Auctor-created illustrative examples**, not official CAT questions. Use them to learn the method, then validate the method on genuine CAT previous-year questions.

## The three-part summary test

| Test | Ask | Reject an option when |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | Does it include the paragraph's governing idea? | It captures only an example or one half of a contrast. |
| Scope | Is it no broader or narrower than the paragraph? | It adds a population, cause or consequence the paragraph never established. |
| Stance | Does it preserve the author's level of certainty? | It turns may into must, criticism into rejection, or observation into advocacy. |

Apply the tests in that order. Coverage quickly removes fragments. Scope removes attractive overreach. Stance usually separates the final two options. This same evidence discipline also matters when you [practise CAT RC accuracy](/features/precision-drills).

## Auctor-created worked example

**Paragraph:** Remote work can widen access to jobs for people outside major cities. Yet access alone does not erase inequality: reliable internet, quiet workspace and managerial trust remain unevenly distributed. Companies that treat location flexibility as a complete inclusion policy may therefore preserve the barriers they intended to remove.

**Option A:** Remote work eliminates geographic and economic inequality in employment.

**Option B:** Although remote work can expand job access, it does not ensure inclusion unless other unequal working conditions are addressed.

**Option C:** Companies should require every employee to work remotely to improve inclusion.

**Option D:** Internet access is the only remaining barrier to equitable employment.

**Answer: B.** It keeps both halves of the argument: expanded access and the limits created by unequal conditions. A overstates the benefit, C invents a recommendation, and D narrows several barriers to one.

## The five traps behind wrong options

1. **Example trap:** repeats a vivid illustration but drops the main claim.
2. **Half-summary trap:** preserves one side of a contrast and ignores the qualification.
3. **Overreach trap:** adds a cause, solution or prediction not established in the paragraph.
4. **Tone shift:** converts a measured observation into praise, alarm or condemnation.
5. **Keyword mirror:** reuses the paragraph's language while changing the relationship between ideas.

These traps overlap with the option errors discussed in [why RC accuracy gets stuck](/blog/improve-rc-accuracy-cat). The difference is that para summary compresses the entire decision into one paragraph-level claim.

## A 60-second solving workflow

### 1. State the paragraph's job

Before reading the options, complete this sentence: “The paragraph argues that…” If you can only name the topic, read again for the relationship between ideas.

### 2. Mark the turn

Words such as *but*, *yet*, *however*, *therefore* and *although* often reveal which claim controls the paragraph. Do not automatically treat the last sentence as the summary; identify what logical work it performs.

### 3. Eliminate by named failure

Do not say an option “feels incomplete.” Name the failure: too narrow, too broad, unsupported conclusion, wrong stance or missing contrast. A named rejection can be reviewed and improved.

### 4. Compare the final two against the paragraph

The better option is not the prettier sentence. It is the one that loses the least essential meaning while adding nothing.

## How to practise without memorising tricks

Build a small error log with four columns: your predicted main claim, the option selected, the exact trap, and the phrase in the paragraph that disproves your choice. Review the log after five sets. If the same trap repeats, use targeted practice before taking another full sectional.

Pair this method with [daily structured VARC practice](/features/daily-rc-workout), and use a [CAT VARC test](/test-series) to check whether the method survives time pressure.

## Final checklist

- Can I express the central claim before viewing options?
- Did I preserve every essential contrast?
- Is the option at the same scope as the paragraph?
- Did it strengthen or weaken the author's certainty?
- Can I name why each rejected option fails?

A good summary is faithful compression. It covers the paragraph's argument, respects its boundaries and keeps its stance intact.
